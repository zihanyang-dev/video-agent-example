# Delivering what you made

**A file in the working directory is not something a person can see.** They are not on this
machine and have no way to open a path. Until you deliver it, all they have is your
description of it.

```bash
skills/deliver/deliver.sh still_water_opener.mp4 final
```

The arguments are the file and what it is:

- `final` — what they asked for. Use this for the cut you would hand over.
- `preview` — something to look at on the way there: a rough assembly, a still from a shot
  you are considering, two versions side by side.

## When to deliver

Deliver the moment something is worth looking at, not only at the end. A person watching a
ten-minute job would rather see the first shot land than read that it is going well.

Deliver again whenever you change it. Each delivery is its own thing on their screen, so a
revision does not replace the version they were looking at — which is what you want when
they are deciding between two cuts.

## After delivering

Say what you did in your own words — what the cut does, what you chose, what you would
change next. Do not describe where the file is or what you ran; they can see the video
itself, and the rest is machinery they are not looking at.
